# State of practice

## Choose this mode when

The question is how something is actually done well today—operations, engineering practice, clinical procedure, safety, governance—and what the field considers standard, emerging, or discredited. Use `technology-landscape` when the subject is a technology rather than a practice; use `policy-analysis` when rules rather than practice are the subject.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. standards, guidelines, and consensus statements from bodies with visible process (and their revision history);
2. post-incident reviews, audits, and retrospectives with method disclosed;
3. practitioner documentation from operators who run the practice at scale (runbooks, engineering blogs with specifics, conference talks with data);
4. surveys of practice with sampling method visible;
5. textbooks and training material, for the taught baseline;
6. vendor best-practice guides and opinion pieces, as claims.

## Decomposition pattern

For each practice element, record: what the standard says, what practitioners report doing, what evidence links the practice to outcomes, and where practice diverges from guidance. Distinguish established, emerging, contested, and deprecated practices explicitly.

## Mode gates

- Guidance is cited to its issuing body and version; superseded guidance is marked as such.
- Practitioner reports name the operating context (scale, sector, constraints) so the reader can judge transfer.
- Outcome claims cite evidence, not consensus alone.

## Output sections

- Verdict
- Established practice
- Emerging and contested practice
- Deprecated or discredited practice
- Evidence linking practice to outcomes
- Context dependence and transfer limits
- Gaps

## Completion criteria

Each practice element is classified with cited guidance and at least one practitioner or outcome source, or is marked as guidance-only; divergence between guidance and practice is visible.

## Pitfalls

- Treating the loudest engineering blog as the field.
- Presenting a vendor's recommended architecture as neutral practice.
- Ignoring the context that made a practice work.
