# Company research

## Choose this mode when

The subject is one organization: its products, business model, execution record, financial and operational position, dependencies, and risks. Use `entity-background` for a lighter identity-and-track-record profile; use `due-diligence` when the work serves a specific transaction with red flags and go/no-go conditions.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. regulatory filings, audited results, and official disclosures;
2. product documentation, pricing pages, terms, and changelogs;
3. customer evidence: case studies with named customers, reviews with method, usage data;
4. litigation, regulatory, and enforcement records;
5. hiring, organizational, and leadership signals with dates;
6. independent reporting with its own sourcing;
7. company communications and executive statements, as primary evidence of what management says.

## Decomposition pattern

Separate what the company says, what it discloses under accounting or regulatory definitions, what customers and third parties observe, and what is inferred. For each strategic claim, name the disclosed metric that would support it and whether it exists. Record dependencies (suppliers, platforms, key customers, regulators).

## Mode gates

- Disclosed metrics carry their definition and period; non-GAAP or adjusted figures are labeled.
- Strategy-success claims cite outcome evidence, not announcements.
- Competitive-advantage claims name the mechanism and the evidence for it.

## Output sections

- Verdict
- Products and business model
- Execution record
- Financial and operational position
- Competitive position and claimed advantages
- Dependencies and risks
- Open questions
- Gaps

## Completion criteria

Every position claim traces to a disclosure, third-party observation, or labeled inference; the dependency map is explicit; management claims are attributed, not adopted.

## Pitfalls

- Treating a press release as proof of product quality.
- Reading hiring as strategy without corroboration.
- Adopting the company's segment definitions without noting them.
