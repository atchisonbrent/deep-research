# Historical analysis

## Choose this mode when

The events are settled enough that the primary record is largely fixed, and the question is what happened, why, how it has been interpreted, and what the record can and cannot support. Use `event-assessment` while the record is still forming.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. archival primary sources: documents, correspondence, official records, contemporaneous data, oral histories with provenance;
2. critical editions and document collections with editorial method stated;
3. peer-reviewed historiography and monographs with visible sourcing;
4. memoirs and retrospective accounts, treated as attributed and interested;
5. popular history, journalism, and encyclopedias, for orientation and leads.

## Decomposition pattern

Separate the documented fact pattern from the interpretive frame. For each causal claim, write: what the sources directly show, what a historian inferred, and where the historiography disagrees. Record the provenance chain for key documents and whether later scholarship has revised earlier readings.

## Mode gates

- Contested interpretations are presented as a historiographical dispute with named positions, not resolved by the report's preference.
- Quotations trace to a primary or critical-edition source, not to a secondary paraphrase.
- Anachronistic framing (judging actors by information they lacked) is flagged where it drives a conclusion.

## Output sections

- Verdict
- Established fact pattern
- Interpretive disputes and their evidence
- Causal assessment
- What the record cannot support
- Sources and provenance notes

## Completion criteria

Fact claims trace to primary or critical-edition sources; interpretive claims name the scholarly position they follow and the strongest rival; the report distinguishes what is settled from what is argued.

## Pitfalls

- Citing a monograph for a fact it took from a document you could have read.
- Treating scholarly consensus as unanimity, or a single revisionist as consensus.
- Motive attribution from outcomes.
