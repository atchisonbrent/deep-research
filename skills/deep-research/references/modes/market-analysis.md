# Market analysis

## Choose this mode when

The question is the size, structure, economics, growth, and competitive dynamics of a market or segment. Use `company-research` for one participant; use `due-diligence` when the analysis serves an investment or acquisition decision with a materiality threshold.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. first-party market data: transaction, pricing, volume, and usage data from exchanges, registries, or operators;
2. regulatory filings and audited financials of participants;
3. government statistics with published methodology;
4. industry datasets whose methodology, definitions, and sampling are inspectable;
5. analyst estimates whose assumptions are inspectable;
6. press coverage and vendor "market size" figures, as claims.

## Decomposition pattern

Fix the market definition first: product boundary, geography, currency, period, and unit. Split into segments. For each quantitative claim record definition, geography, currency, period, and source method. Separate observed economics from narrative. Build scenarios with named drivers instead of a single forecast.

## Mode gates

- No figure is combined with another unless definition, geography, currency, and period are reconciled explicitly.
- Size is a range with basis, not one number.
- Growth claims distinguish volume, price, and mix effects where the data allows.

## Output sections

- Verdict
- Market definition and boundary
- Size range and basis
- Segments and structure
- Economics and drivers
- Competitive structure
- Scenarios and sensitivities
- Forecast triggers
- Gaps

## Completion criteria

The market definition is explicit; every figure carries its four qualifiers; scenarios name drivers and what would distinguish them; the analyst-estimate dependence is mapped.

## Pitfalls

- Averaging TAM figures built on different definitions.
- Treating an analyst's forecast as data.
- Letting the vendor with the widest category definition set the frame.
