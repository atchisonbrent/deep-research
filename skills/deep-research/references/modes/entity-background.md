# Entity background

## Choose this mode when

The question is who or what an organization, project, or public-role individual is: identity, affiliations, track record, and credibility, usually to decide whether to trust, engage, cite, or rely on them. Use `company-research` for a full business analysis; use `due-diligence` for a transaction.

**Scope boundary.** This mode covers organizations, projects, and individuals acting in a public or professional capacity relevant to the question (an author's beat, a maintainer's history, an executive's record). It does not cover private individuals, private life, or anything that would amount to surveillance or doxxing. Record only what is public, relevant to the stated purpose, and attributable. Stop and state the boundary if the request drifts.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. official registries and records: incorporation, charity, professional licensing, court dockets, patent and trademark offices;
2. the entity's own primary output: publications, code, filings, talks, with dates;
3. independent reporting and reviews with sourcing;
4. affiliations stated by the affiliating body (employer page, board listing, conference committee);
5. self-description (bios, about pages, profiles), as attributed;
6. social media, as evidence only of what was posted and when.

## Decomposition pattern

Separate: verified identity (this is the same entity across sources); stated versus independently confirmed affiliations; track record as dated, checkable artifacts; documented conflicts or controversies with primary sources; and what is not established.

## Mode gates

- Identity is resolved across sources before any track-record claim is attached; name collisions are treated as unresolved.
- Every credential or affiliation is confirmed by the credentialing or affiliating body or marked self-reported.
- Controversy claims cite a primary record or named reporting, never aggregation sites alone.
- Purpose and relevance are stated for every included fact.

## Output sections

- Verdict (fit for the stated purpose)
- Identity resolution
- Affiliations and roles (confirmed vs self-reported)
- Track record
- Documented conflicts or controversies
- Not established
- Gaps

## Completion criteria

Identity is resolved or explicitly unresolved; each affiliation and credential has a confirmation status; track record items are dated artifacts; the report contains nothing outside the stated purpose.

## Pitfalls

- Merging two people or organizations with the same name.
- Treating a self-written bio as confirmation.
- Letting a controversy aggregator substitute for the primary record.
- Drifting from professional relevance into private life.
